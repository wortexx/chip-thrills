# Thesis review checklist

Work top-down. Big structural problems make polishing pointless, so report them first. For each finding give: location, problem, why it matters, concrete fix.

## A. Research problem and contribution
- [ ] Can you state the research problem / questions in one or two sentences after reading the introduction? Are they stated explicitly?
- [ ] Is it clear why the problem matters (motivation) and by what criteria a solution is judged?
- [ ] Is the author's own contribution explicit, and separated from prior work, supervisors' or the team's work, and reused code/IP? Ideally 2–4 listed achievements.
- [ ] Is the novelty stated and positioned relative to prior work?
- [ ] Does the title contain the key search terms and only claims (fast, efficient…) that are substantiated?

## B. Structure — each part doing its job
- [ ] Introduction: motivation, objectives, brief prior-work placement, contribution, chapter roadmap. No detailed methods/results/conclusions.
- [ ] Background: only what's needed; concepts defined; sources compared not just summarised; summary table where many similar items are surveyed; every concept used later.
- [ ] Methods/materials: reproducible (tools and versions, settings, data, setup, repetitions); method choice justified against alternatives; link from background visible.
- [ ] Results: presented with figures/tables, discussed, compared with prior work; error sources, deviations, validity and reliability addressed; objectives checked.
- [ ] Conclusions: main results and their importance, key numbers repeated, research questions answered, recommendations, limitations, future work. No new results.
- [ ] Abstract: one page, self-contained, problem–method–results (with numbers)–conclusions; no citations, figures, tables; nothing not in the body; 3–5 keywords by importance.
- [ ] Abstract, introduction and conclusions promise and deliver the same things (same numbers, same contribution list).
- [ ] Chapters 2–4 have descriptive titles; Introduction and Conclusions keep their names.
- [ ] Logical flow: no concept used before it's defined; chapters open with what they cover (consistently); proportions match importance.
- [ ] Skim test: headings + figures + tables alone tell the story.

## C. Argumentation and evidence
- [ ] Every comparative or evaluative claim (better, faster, cheaper, efficient, common, widely used) has a number and/or reference.
- [ ] Key terms are operationally defined (how is "performance"/"efficiency" measured? is bigger better?).
- [ ] Comparisons change one variable at a time, or acknowledge confounders; measurement noise/variance is reported.
- [ ] No causal claims from correlation; no generalisation from too few observations; no "always/never" without proof.
- [ ] Alternative explanations and contrary evidence considered; limitations acknowledged.
- [ ] No contradictions between sections.
- [ ] No marketing language.

## D. Figures, tables, equations, code
- [ ] Every figure/table is referenced in the text (before or near it) and discussed — what should the reader notice? Some numbers repeated in prose.
- [ ] Figure captions below, table captions above; captions are informative.
- [ ] No chapter/section starts with a figure, table or list.
- [ ] Plots: axes labelled with quantity and unit, readable fonts (≥ ~10 pt), scale from zero unless justified, log scales marked, greyscale-safe, no unnecessary elements.
- [ ] Tables: deliberate row/column order, units in headers, numbers right/decimal-aligned, consistent powers of ten, minimal rules, best values highlighted where comparing.
- [ ] Borrowed figures cited in captions ("adapted from [n]" if modified); reuse rights considered.
- [ ] Equations punctuated as part of sentences, numbered if referenced, all symbols defined at first use, no symbol reused for two meanings, functions upright and variables italic, log bases given, ratios defined.
- [ ] Code: monospaced, line-numbered when referenced, short inline / medium as numbered listing / long in appendix; described in text.

## E. References
- [ ] One citation style, applied consistently; numeric and name-year not mixed.
- [ ] Every cited work in the bibliography and every bibliography entry cited.
- [ ] Numeric citations don't start sentences; citations placed before the full stop, close to the claim; extent of multi-sentence citations unambiguous.
- [ ] Sources are reliable (peer-reviewed preferred; no Wikipedia; arXiv replaced by published versions where they exist); several sources for important background claims.
- [ ] Entries complete: all authors, title, venue in full and consistent form, year, volume/issue/pages, DOI/URL with access date for online material, document IDs for standards/datasheets.
- [ ] Secondary citations marked as such; direct quotes verbatim with page numbers.
- [ ] No passages closely paraphrasing a source without citation (plagiarism risk) — including the author's own earlier papers.

## F. Language and mechanics
- [ ] Short sentences, active verbs, no circumlocution or filler ("it can be seen that", "in order to", "very", "really", "quite").
- [ ] No "I" outside the preface; consistent choice of "we"/passive.
- [ ] Abbreviations defined at first use, not overused; consistent capitalisation; e.g./i.e. preferably written out.
- [ ] Terminology consistent (same thing, same word throughout).
- [ ] Numbers as numerals with units: space between number and unit (non-breaking), leading zeros, thousands separators, en dash for ranges.
- [ ] Lists: introduced by text, parallel items, deliberate order, numbered when referenced.
- [ ] Headings: ≤ 3 levels, ≤ 5–6 words, no lone subsections, ≥ 2 paragraphs per (sub)section, no one-sentence paragraphs.
- [ ] Cross-references by number ("Chapter 3", "Section 4.2"), generated automatically.
- [ ] No leftover TODOs, placeholders, commented-out text visible in the PDF, "??" from broken refs, or overfull boxes running into the margin.
- [ ] Spelling and grammar checked; one variety of English throughout.

## G. Integrity and admin
- [ ] Preface states supervision and contributions; no criticism of anyone.
- [ ] Company-confidential material has permission.
- [ ] Institution-required statements present (originality check, AI-use disclosure, declaration of authorship) — per local rules.
